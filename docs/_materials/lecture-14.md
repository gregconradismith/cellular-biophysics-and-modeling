# Square-wave bursting and homoclinic bifurcations

## Required Reading

- <span style="color: var(--color-text);">Read the highlighted parts of <strong>Izhikevich 2000</strong> (a review of neuronal bursting mechanisms).</span>\
  \
  <span style="color: var(--color-text);">You may be amazed at how much you understand about the nonlinear dynamics of neuronal activity. The three most important types of bursting are:</span>\
  - <span style="color: var(--color-text);"><strong>Square-wave bursting</strong> - also called <strong>fold/homoclinic bursting</strong> - onset is a saddle-node (fold) bifurcation, offset is a saddle homoclinic orbit bifurcation, and the fast subsystem is bistable.  </span>
  - <span style="color: var(--color-text);"><strong>Elliptic bursting</strong> - also called <strong>subHopf/fold cycle bursting</strong> - onset is a subcritical Hopf bifurcation, offset is a fold limit cycle bifurcation, and the fast subsystem is bistable.  </span>
  - <span style="color: var(--color-text);"><strong>Parabolic bursting</strong> - also called <strong>circle/circle bursting</strong> - onset is a SNIC bifurcation, offset is also a SNIC bifurcation, and the fast subsystem is not bistable</span>\
- The highlighted portions of <strong>Bertram et al. 2007</strong> on the control of insulin secretion by pancreatic beta-cells through metabolic and electrical oscillations.\
- Highlighted portions of <strong>Tabak et al. 2000</strong> on network bursting in embryonic chick spinal cord.
