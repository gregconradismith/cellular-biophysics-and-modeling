Cellular Biophysics and Modeling is built around one idea: biological systems become more intelligible when we can describe how they change. The course treats electrophysiology, excitable membranes, ion channels, and neural dynamics as scientific phenomena that can be reasoned about with equations, graphs, physical intuition, and careful language.

<div class="philosophy-quote">
  <blockquote>
    "One who chops their own firewood warms themselves twice."
  </blockquote>
</div>

That proverb is the spirit of the course. Homework, practice exams, hand-drawn graphs, and handwritten calculations are not just things to submit; they are the primary way students learn the material. The work itself is the preparation.

The course is intentionally quantitative and integrated. Rather than separating biology from mathematics, it asks students to move back and forth between them: a current-voltage relation becomes a circuit model; a channel gating variable becomes an ordinary differential equation; a cell's behavior becomes a phase plane; a biological question becomes a model whose assumptions can be inspected.

The course is also intentionally open. Past assignments, exams, notes, examples, and supporting materials are part of the learning environment. Students are encouraged to study them deliberately, use them to monitor progress, and return to them when a concept needs another pass.

Collaboration is encouraged, but transparency matters. Students should say what people, tools, texts, examples, and generative AI systems helped them arrive at an answer. The final submitted work should be their own handwritten expression of their understanding, not a copied final product.

Generative AI can be useful for clarification, review, and exploration, but it should not replace the intellectual exercise. If an assignment is meant to help you consolidate an idea, outsourcing that struggle short-circuits the learning. The goal is not merely to have an answer; the goal is to become the sort of person who can reason through the answer.

The course is demanding because the subject is demanding. Excitable cells are physical, chemical, electrical, and dynamical systems all at once. Learning to see those layers together takes repetition, patience, and honest practice. That is why the course values steady work, clear notation, careful diagrams, and the willingness to revise one's understanding.

In short: do the problems, draw the pictures, ask where the equations came from, and let the mathematics sharpen the biology.
